# Core clarity rubric

Use this rubric to separate three failures that are often collapsed into “the game is unclear.” It is a diagnostic frame, not evidence that a game is fun, commercially viable, or failing for one specific reason.

## 1. Distinctiveness

Describe the shortest repeated player action as `action → system response → readable consequence`. Genre combinations, content quantity, item count, and production polish are not a distinctive system by themselves.

- `visible`: the operated task demonstrates a specific mechanism whose response changes the next decision.
- `partial`: a potentially distinctive mechanism exists, but the task does not expose a meaningful consequence or repeated choice.
- `not-observed`: the declared mechanism should have appeared in this task but did not.
- `not-assessable`: the task or evidence cannot test the mechanism.

Ideas borrowed from another game remain candidates until the meaningful difference in action, response, and reward is explicit. Do not recommend surface copying.

## 2. Communication

Test whether the same core can be understood from the first relevant exposure. A logo, genre label, feature list, or cinematic cut-in cannot substitute for showing the distinctive action and its consequence.

Record what the stimulus actually shows, what remains implicit, and the earliest moment the action-response promise becomes legible. Do not infer trailer abandonment, conversion, or purchase intent without eligible audience evidence.

## 3. Scene legibility

For the main play state, check whether a first-time viewer can separate:

- the controlled actor and current target;
- threat, interactable, and background;
- world objects and HUD;
- current state, state change, and result;
- the next available action.

Camera, framing, scale, color separation, effects, text density, HP representation, and motion hierarchy are possible causes to test—not universal prescriptions. A static image can support visible hierarchy findings but cannot prove motion, latency, input feel, or hidden state.

## 4. Evidence contract

Report the three axes separately with `visible`, `partial`, `not-observed`, or `not-assessable`, a concrete finding, and evidence IDs. Keep one shared operated stimulus across the neutral pass and every grounded player lens. A review-derived predicted response remains a hypothesis and needs a neutral human falsifier.
